# Teaching Pablo in about four minutes

## Do the setup silently, then say this

"Four cards each, face down. Look at two of them now — and only now."

Let everyone peek. The moment they put them back, the game has already taught
itself its central idea: you know two, you have forgotten one, and the fourth
was never yours to know.

## Then the turn

"Draw a card. Either swap it for one of yours, or throw it away and use its
power. You want your four cards to add up to as little as possible."

## Say before dealing, not after

Read out the card powers you are using, and the penalty for a failed call.
There is no publisher for this game — Pablo, Cabo and Cambio are the same game
with different local rules — so there is genuinely nothing to appeal to
mid-round. Thirty seconds now saves the argument.

## Emphasise once

"You are trying to score **low**." Somebody always spends the first round
collecting high cards.

## Consider dropping

The snap rule, if anyone at the table is slower to react. It is fun and it is
entirely optional, and it is the one rule that can make a player feel bad.
