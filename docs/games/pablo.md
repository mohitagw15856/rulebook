## Rules

Also played as **Cabo** and **Cambio**. Four cards in front of you, face down,
and you are allowed to look at two of them — once, at the start. Everything
after that is memory.

## Setup

Deal four cards face down to each player, arranged in a two-by-two square. Do
not look at them.

Each player may then look at **exactly two** of their own four cards, once. Then
they go back face down and stay that way.

Place the rest of the deck face down as the stock and turn one card to start the
discard pile.

## Playing a turn

Draw one card, either from the stock or the top of the discard pile. Then choose:

- **Swap** it for one of your four face-down cards, putting the old one on the
  discard pile. You may look at the card you are putting down, but not at the
  one you are picking up if it came from the stock.
- **Discard** it directly, using its power if it has one.

## Card powers

There is no publisher and therefore no official set. The most common is:

| Card | Power |
| --- | --- |
| 7 or 8 | Look at one of your own cards |
| 9 or 10 | Look at one card belonging to another player |
| Jack or Queen | Swap any two cards on the table, without looking |
| Black King | Worth zero points |

**Agree the set before dealing.** Every group plays this differently and every
group assumes theirs is standard.

## Calling Pablo

Instead of drawing, you may call "Pablo" if you believe you have the lowest
total. Every other player takes one final turn, then all cards are revealed.

- **You are lowest** — you score zero for the round.
- **You are not** — you take a penalty, most commonly 10 points plus your hand.

## Scoring

Number cards count face value, Jacks and Queens count 10, Kings count 10 except
the black Kings which count zero, and Aces count 1.

Play rounds until somebody passes 100 points. **Lowest** cumulative score wins —
this is a game you are trying to lose.

## Settle the argument

### Is Pablo the same game as Cabo?

**Official rule.** Played by almost everyone, almost everywhere.

Effectively yes. Pablo, Cabo and Cambio are the same game under different names, with small local differences in card powers and penalties. Four-card Golf is a close relative without the special cards. There is no single publisher, which is precisely why every group's version differs.

```console
$ rulebook ruling pablo "is pablo the same as cabo"
```

### Which cards have powers?

**Not an official rule.** Played by almost everyone, almost everywhere.

There is no single authority, so no set is official. The most widely used set is below, and it is worth stating out loud before dealing.

*The house version:* Discarding a 7 or 8 lets you look at one of your own cards; a 9 or 10 lets you look at another player's card; a Jack or Queen lets you swap two cards blind; a black King is worth zero. Some groups let the Queen look and then choose whether to swap.

*What it changes:* Peek powers reward memory; blind-swap powers reward aggression. Groups that use both play a noticeably sharper game.

```console
$ rulebook ruling pablo "what do the cards do in pablo"
```

### What happens if you call Pablo and are not lowest?

**Not an official rule.** Played by almost everyone, almost everywhere.

Universally penalised, but the size varies.

*The house version:* The most common penalty is a flat 10 points added to the caller's score, sometimes plus their hand. Some groups instead double the caller's total.

*What it changes:* A large penalty makes calling rare and the game longer; a small one turns it into a race. Agree it before the first round.

```console
$ rulebook ruling pablo "failed pablo call"
```

### Can you get rid of a card by matching the discard?

**Not an official rule.** Widespread but far from universal.

A widespread addition rather than a base rule.

*The house version:* At any time, if the top of the discard pile matches the rank of one of your face-down cards, you may snap it onto the pile and be rid of it. Snap wrongly and you take a penalty card.

*What it changes:* Rewards memory sharply and speeds the game up, but disadvantages anyone slower to react. Drop it if that matters at your table.

```console
$ rulebook ruling pablo "snapping pablo"
```

## Teaching it

## Do the setup silently, then say this

"Four cards each, face down. Look at two of them now — and only now."

Let everyone peek. The moment they put them back, the game has already taught
itself its central idea: you know two, you have forgotten one, and the fourth
was never yours to know.

## Then the turn

"Draw a card. Either swap it for one of yours, or throw it away and use its
power. You want your four cards to add up to as little as possible."

## Say before dealing, not after

Read out the card powers you are using, and the penalty for a failed call.
There is no publisher for this game — Pablo, Cabo and Cambio are the same game
with different local rules — so there is genuinely nothing to appeal to
mid-round. Thirty seconds now saves the argument.

## Emphasise once

"You are trying to score **low**." Somebody always spends the first round
collecting high cards.

## Consider dropping

The snap rule, if anyone at the table is slower to react. It is fun and it is
entirely optional, and it is the one rule that can make a player feel bad.

## When a piece goes missing

Any deck. Without jokers, drop their power and treat Kings as the zero-value card, which many groups do anyway.

## Accessibility

Relies on memory rather than speed, but the snap rule rewards quick hands and can exclude players with slower reaction times — it is easily dropped without harming the game. Card values are numeric, so colour vision is not required.
